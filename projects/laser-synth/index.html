Overview

Modern sound synthesis offers infinite sonic possibilities, yet digital instruments often lack the physical expressiveness of their acoustic counterparts. The Laser Synth is a Virtual Reality (VR) instrument developed as my Bachelor's capstone project and presented at the ICSC 2024 Csound Conference.

Unlike VR installations that rely on generative randomness, the Laser Synth is designed for intentional, traditional performance. By utilizing spatial tracking in a 3D environment, the instrument allows performers to manipulate pitch, timbre, and complex modulation through physical hand gestures, creating an interactable interface that bridges human movement with the infinite possibilities of digital audio in a live performance context.

Instrument Control & Design

The instrument architecture centers on three objects: a stationary central crystal and two hand-held orbs. The lasers which connect these object represent the geometric relationship that drives the synthesis engine.

Pitch & Filter

The distance between the Crystal and right hand controls pitch (quantized to 12-TET), while the distance between the Two Orbs modulates the filter cutoff frequency. This spatial mapping makes complex synthesis changes as natural as opening or closing one's arms.

XY-Pad Rotation

The left hand's rotation functions as a 3D XY-pad, allowing for the simultaneous manipulation of up to four parameters like vibrato, reverb, and distortion through fluid wrist movements.

Technical Implementation

The Laser Synth leverages the CsoundUnity package to run Csound, a high-performance audio engine, within Unity, a real-time 3D environment. This decoupling allows Unity to focus on spatial logic and visual feedback while Csound handles precise sound synthesis.

The Csound code was developed and refined in Cabbage which provided a rapid prototyping environment before integration into Unity. To handle the complexities of VR interaction, I integrated the Unity package Auto Hand VR. This implemented VR into Unity in a way that makes intuitive, tactile control over the synth's modular elements.

Key Outcomes

  • Natural Interaction: Proved that spatial distances in VR provide a more intuitive "theremin-like" control for synthesis than traditional knobs and sliders.
  • Visual Reinforcement: Developed a HUD including a 3D chromatic tuner and dynamic laser color-coding to provide the performer with the necessary feedback for precise live performance.
  • Academic Recognition: Successfully presented at the International Csound Conference (ICSC) 2024, highlighting the intersection of VR and professional-grade synthesis.

Challenges & Solutions

3D Pitch Accuracy

Performing melodies in an invisible 3D space is notoriously difficult, as there is no haptic feedback or "fret" to guide the hand.

Solution: Implemented a 12-tone equal temperament quantization engine within the Unity-to-Csound pipeline. This provided a "margin of error," allowing the performer to hit the desired frequency while maintaining a smooth, expressive feel.

Sound Design Testing

When creting a Csound instrument for the system, needing to test within VR then take off the VR headset to adjust the code was cumbersome.

Solution: Introduced simple dropdown and slider controls within the VR environment to adjust 10 key parameters, allowing for basic sound design tweaks without removing the headset.

Future Considerations

The current iteration of Laser Synth serves as a proof-of-concept for immersive audio manipulation. The project identifies several key areas where the framework could be expanded:

Scalable Polyphony

A primary goal for future iterations would be the transition from monophonic to polyphonic synthesis. This could be achieved by manifesting multiple resonant crystals as independent audio voices, allowing for complex harmonic layering and multi-object manipulation within the 3D environment.

Expanded Preset Architecture

To lower the barrier to entry, a future design goal would include a comprehensive preset ecosystem. Providing high-quality "out-of-the-box" configurations would ensure immediate sonic gratification for new users, while allowing advanced performers to capture, recall, and toggle between custom parameter states during live use.
